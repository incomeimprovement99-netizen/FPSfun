# 26 September 2026

The owner changed the game's direction: the project becomes **SpeedKills**, a fast, vertical,
futuristic-city shooter and battle royale, built on everything made so far rather than started again.
The brief was 62 sections; its first ask was to keep today's game safe.

**Frozen first.** The game as it stood, Milestones 1 to 182, is the git tag `apex-era-final`. It went to
the game server and to Pages through the release gate, with the picture test passing, so the live site is
that build. Local work that was not ready went to a branch of its own, `wip/follower-graph`. Two of the
owner's local files could not be committed from this session and are still where they were; a personal
email draft was left out of the repository on purpose.

**Read before changing.** Four passes over the code found that the machinery is general and the design is
written into lists and branches:

- One resolver makes every gun, and nothing downstream cares which gun it is. The choice of guns sits in
  about fifteen hand-written lists.
- The abilities are if-chains through `main.ts`.
- The battle royale map is wired in by constants in about thirty places.
- A player's life is a dozen booleans. The passes found eight ways those can contradict each other. One of
  them lets a late network packet bring a dead player back on someone else's screen.

That shaped the plan, `docs/PHASE_18_PLAN_SPEEDKILLS.md`:

- One game switch.
- One profile per game, the legacy one being today's lists moved over unchanged.
- Registries in place of the if-chains.
- A map registry with a collision grid and a bot graph that knows floors.
- A pure state machine for life, death, the Gulag and the ghost.
- Twelve phases after the brief's own order.

**The first footing** (Milestone 183): the switch, both profiles and a check that holds each to naming only
things that exist. Nothing reads the switch yet, so the legacy game cannot have changed.

The rest waits for the owner's yes on the architecture, as the brief asked, and on ten design decisions that
are theirs.

## The owner's answers, and the build

Twenty questions, answered, then the plan approved with the owner's own numbers: 6 to 7 minute matches, a
biggest and hottest centre sector the decay closes toward, fusion at 2% damage and 10% magazine a level.
Then one long run, stage after stage (Milestones 184 to 187):

- SpeedKills is now what the site opens in; the legacy game is a link away and the test suite still guards it.
- Movement for roofs: double jump, wall run and auto-climb for everyone, lighter, and a two-storey roof in
  reach. A cap on how far a climb may rise was the real limit, found by tracing a climb frame by frame.
- Ten guns in pairs, five named for the owner's friends, tuned by a check to fights of about 1.5 s, fusion to
  level 5, infinite ammo, and a shield that comes back on its own.
- Ten hacks, with Hyper Scape's own published cooldowns where it published them.

The owner asked, mid-run, that the phases and milestones stay written up, that Hyper Scape's exact numbers be
used, and that the battle royale be full of buildings, dark and neo-futuristic, from more free assets. The
city is next.

## The ghost, and the controller

**Stage I** (Milestone 191): no knockdowns, the Gulag first, then a ghost. The ghost follows its squad to be
restored at its echo, 5 s beside it, three times slower away from it, twice a match.

**Stage M** (Milestone 192): the controller made first class.

- Its numbers moved into `gamepad.json`.
- An outer deadzone and a curve strength in Settings.
- Per-optic ADS on the advanced look.
- Aim assist that fades with distance and never snaps from one target to another.
- A check that no SpeedKills key does two things.

Each new check was proven by putting its bug back.

## Bots

**Stage N, first part** (Milestone 193).

- Five bot tiers, with a new Beginner to learn on.
- SpeedKills names in the menu.
- Bots on a player's 100 and 50, with regeneration. Before this, a bot in the city could loot its way to 125
  shield and never heal.
- Heal and Dash hacks for bots, by tier.

## Thirty in the city

**Stage K** (Milestone 194). Measured with a new CPU profiler before anything was changed. The city's
triangles were never the cost. Three things were:

- three.js walking hidden objects every frame;
- every bot casting a sight ray at everyone every frame;
- see-through glass drawn in two passes, with a shader rebuild before each.

After the fixes the city runs at 200 fps in the street and 213 over the Spire on Competitive, from 133 and
96. The legacy match went from 250 to 345.

## The tour, and what the release run found

**Stage O** (Milestone 195). SpeedKills' tour is eight steps, and a new e2e section does each one for real.

**The release run** (Milestone 196) found two real bugs:

- a supply bin offered through the vault's shut door;
- bots stranding against walls when frames ran slow.

The second only showed on a loaded machine. A new CPU-throttle switch for the e2e made it fail on demand,
and walking a bot's step in pieces of at most 0.2 m fixed it. Both fixes are proven by putting the bug
back.

## An arena from the city

**Stage L** (Milestone 198): NEON BLOCK, a street crossing at night, with decks a storey up and skybridges.
SpeedKills' arena modes are fought there. The arena checks caught three faults in the first draft, each
fixed. The README now has a SpeedKills section saying what is in it.

## Released, and the city's sound

The build with stages I, M, N, K and O went out: the release run's only failure (the host migration setup)
passed alone. It is live on the game server and on Pages. After it: an arena cut from the city (NEON
BLOCK), the gun panel's fusion pips, a Gulag loss that is no longer a second death, and a night-city ambience
under every SpeedKills match.

## The third release

The movement lab, the ridden dropship check, bots taking the roofs (and the overlapping towers their routes
found), a revive credited only when it happens, and one count of a player's deaths went out as 6538f98.

The full run had twelve failures. Its first batch shared the machine with my own checks, and eleven of them
passed alone. The twelfth was real, in a test: the roof climb's tower was outside the first decay wave but
its street node was not, so the bot ran from the decay and gave the climb up. The check now picks a route that
is wholly outside it. Mixed-version play against the live build passed with the down message's new field.

## Downtown, from the centre out

The owner's ask: the modes are training; the map is the game. Build the downtown from the centre out, study
Hyper Scape, rank the gaps and build them in order. A research pass (reviews and eight official screenshots)
went into `docs/PHASE_19_PLAN_DOWNTOWN.md` with the ranked steps.

Steps 1 and 2 are in (Milestone 209): the core rebuilt as podiums and towers with canyons a double jump clears,
the Spire in tiers, and Hyper Scape-style jump pads on every block, gold rings on beams of light. Getting a pad
to land you on a 48 m roof took three tries; the fourth splits the throw into straight up, then over the edge
once you are above it.
