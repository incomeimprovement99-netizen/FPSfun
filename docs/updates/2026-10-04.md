# 2026-10-04

**A battle royale on the city loads again.** Yesterday's loading change used a helper that broke in the live build, so
matches got stuck at the end of UNPACKING THE CITY. It is fixed, and loading changes are now tested on the live build's
kind before they go out.

**More tips while you load in.** The tips beside your player card are no longer cut off at four: the column fills to
the bottom (nine on a 1080p screen), and long names on the card stay on one line.

**The city downloads once, not every visit.** Your browser was throwing away the city's big file (79 to 137 MB) between
visits, so the first match of every visit downloaded it again. It is now kept, and a return visit goes straight to
unpacking it. The page's first download is also a quarter smaller: the city's collision comes in after the range is up.

**The USSO and BOOG held like Hyper Scape's.** Both guns sit where you picked them: a little nearer your eye, the front turned right, still pointing at the crosshair. The USSO's left hand now holds it low, just ahead of the trigger, as the Harpy is held, coming up from the bottom of the screen with only the hand and a little wrist showing. BOOG's left arm goes down out of the picture and its hand holds the fore-end ahead of the magazine. Reloads, the melee, the inspect, swaps and pickups all still keep the hands out of the guns.

**Golden hour is the default sky now**, and the sky files are a fifth of the size (the first screen waited for one).
The game's code also downloads about a quarter smaller: the server sends it compressed with brotli.

**Other players now see REZ held properly.** The auto shotgun is the fourth new gun fitted on the soldier other players
see: the left hand on the front of the gun ahead of its magazine, the trigger finger on the trigger, and carried
properly when standing, aiming, sprinting and jumping.

**A little less work for each far-away player.** Players more than 60 metres away no longer draw their eyes, which are
far smaller than a pixel at that distance. In a busy city street that saves about 18 drawing steps a frame.

**The big building, checked floor by floor.** Walked from the bottom up after the owner's play test. The tower's floors
had a ragged crack all round where the floor and ceiling met the outer wall, with the sky showing through; the floors now
reach the wall and the crack is gone. Every stair in the building was measured tread by tread and none runs into a
ceiling, and every open window can be shot and jumped through. At street level Noodle Row has a canopy of red lanterns
strung across its streets, Market has stacks of crates, and lamps ring the Well's shaft.

**The range builds faster.** Its walls, rails and course pieces are rounded boxes, and making each new size used to
take a slow general-purpose step; it is now worked out once and reused, a third of the time.

**A friends' match on the city no longer drops a slow side.** If one of you was still loading the city's walls when
the other joined, the match could end at once with "The host left the match." The waiting side now keeps the
connection alive and catches up when it is ready.

**The vault glows gold.** A third fresh review scored the centre 5 of 10. THE VAULT's storey now has a band of gold light
round the tower at its floor and its ceiling, so you can see from anywhere where the best loot is; the zip lines are
twice as thick; and the strip along the foot of the tower's walls is straight instead of jagged.

**Only SpeedKills.** Joining a friend whose page was still on the old game used to switch you into the old game, old
name and all, and the switch never got you into their match anyway. Now you are told their page is behind and asked to
have them reload and send a new code.

**The page opens a little faster again.** The robot figures in the range and on the courses were each built piece by
piece; now each kind is built once and copied, and the first screen goes about an eighth of a second sooner. Bots in a
match are made the same way, three times faster each.

**"No match with that code" fixed.** For a few minutes after an update, a friend joining your code could be told there
was no such match: if your page was slow to ask the server where to register the code, it quietly used a public
matchmaking server instead of ours, and your friend looked on ours. Your page now waits for our server's answer.

**The streets are harder to snipe down.** A new tool stands an eye at head height on every bit of street and looks 48
ways: on the old map, from nine street points in ten someone could see you from past 60 m. It was not empty blocks but
the roads themselves lining up into 200 m straights. Now low buildings stand where the ground was free, tall advertising
boards stand in the median, every second parked car is a hover van, and the edge road has parked cars: the share of
directions you can be seen from far off fell from 8.1% to 4.5% on the curves and from 13% to 7.3% on the Loop.

**CHOOCH held properly, and other players see it overheat.** The energy LMG is the fifth new gun fitted on the soldier
other players see. When it overheats, other players now see the soldier tip the gun up and hold it while it cools,
instead of pretending to change a magazine it does not have. Bots carrying CHOOCH do the same.

**Swapping no longer pushes the gun into your hands.** While the gun phased out and the next one in, your cupped hands stayed still as the gun kept gently breathing, and BOOG could sway a few millimetres into your fingers. The hands now move with the gun.

**APUHTHEE turned the right way round.** The heavy pistol was being held backwards, by you and by everyone else: aimed,
you were looking down its barrel. The tool that measures the bought guns decides which end is the front from where the
model's centre point sits, and on this pistol that point is almost exactly in the middle, so it guessed wrong by 2 mm.
It now looks at where the grip hangs when the centre point cannot tell, and only this pistol changed.

**The game opens faster again.** The sky was being fetched late, so the game drew the world once without it and then
redrew everything with it. The sky now comes first and everything is prepared once: the first screen is about a fifth
of a second sooner, and everything is in about 0.4 s sooner.

**The High City can be climbed.** Two of the four High City blocks now have a column of balconies up their face toward
the Loop, a jump and a double jump apart, from the street to the deck at 27 m: a way up that is not a lift or a pad.
The other two blocks' faces are too round for it yet. A try at hiding the empty ground past the edge road with the
pack's fake building fronts was dropped: they turned out to be see-through screens.

**The tower's floors look built, not painted.** The coloured walls inside the big tower were a single flat colour over a
grainy texture. They now wear the pack's wall panels in the same colours, green, black, orange and blue, so each floor
still reads as itself.

**The decay now takes the Neon City piece by piece.** The battle royale's nine sectors were still the old city's, which put
the whole Neon City in the middle one: matches decayed empty ground for three waves and then everything at once, and
most drop spots were out past the edge. The Neon City now has its own nine, its tower, its four High City decks and its
four corner blocks, so it closes in district by district and every drop lands in the city.

**Bots now fight for the capture zone on the Neon City.** When a match's last sector opened its capture zone, the bots
were trying to walk to a spot they could never reach, a roof or a cellar, so they just stood still. They now head for the
nearest spot they can actually get to inside the zone, and the tower's zone moved into the base's hall where they can
follow.

**Battle royales start without the long freeze.** Starting a match froze the screen for over a second while the game
placed every item of loot, checking each spot against every wall in the city. It now only checks the walls nearby, with
exactly the same loot in exactly the same places, and the player card's picture is prepared before you need it.

**APUHTHEE held in both hands.** Other players now see the heavy pistol held the way a pistol is: out in front in
both hands, the left wrapped round the right, raised to the eye when aiming, with the trigger finger on the trigger. It
had dangled from the right hand in a one-handed pose. Bots carrying it look the same.

**The four High City blocks now each look like their own.** The review said the four big blocks on stilts were one
building copied four times. Each one's frames and lit trims are now in its own colour, the same as its lift and its
part of the map in a battle royale: the north block cyan, the east pink, the south amber and the west green, so you
can tell from the street which one you are under.

**No more supply bins.** The weapon cases you held E at to open are gone from the battle royale: the floor's guns and
hack cores are where the loot is.

**Headshots from behind.** Other players' soldiers used to drop their heads with their chests, chin nearly on the chest
when crouching or sprinting, so from behind a shot at the head you could see mostly hit the neck or the back instead:
crouched or sprinting, fewer than one in ten counted as a headshot. The soldier now keeps its head up, looking where it
aims (or straight ahead), and from behind nine in ten shots at the head you can see are headshots.

**Soldiers crouch and sprint more upright.** Other players' soldiers no longer hunch over when crouching to aim or
sprinting, so from behind you see more of their head, nearly twice as much in those poses, and shots at it count.

**More to hide behind in the streets.** The review said the streets were still too open. Planters, tall advertising
boards and kiosks now stand along every street round the centre, so wherever you are on a street there is something to
duck behind within about 6 metres. Before, a third of the street was further than that from anything, and in places 20
metres. And two things that lied to you are gone: the barriers down the middle of the streets were see-through railings
that still stopped bullets, so you could see someone through the bars and not hit them (they are planters now), and the
advertising boards were blank and see-through from behind (they now have posters on both sides).

**No more death boxes.** When someone dies in a battle royale, what they carried now falls loose on the floor where they
fell, for anyone to pick up. In a squad they also leave an echo, a glowing marker: a squad mate holds E at it for seven
seconds and they stand up there with the guns and gear they died with, even if someone has taken the loose copies
meanwhile. The banners, the respawn beacons and the box itself are gone.

**A simpler Play menu.** Battle Royale is now the big card at the top, with the Firing Range under it. Every other
mode is one click away behind **Extra modes**. The match's options are tucked behind **Adjust settings** on the
right: leave it shut and you play on the usual defaults, open it and everything that was there before is there.

**The range's big screen loads when you use it.** The manual on the screen at the end of the range is no longer part
of the game's first download: the screen shows THE MANUAL and asks you to shoot it, and the text arrives the moment
you do. It is a small saving for SpeedKills players, and it takes 158 KB out of the file every visitor loads first.

**Grenades are gone.** SpeedKills never had them, but the game still loaded and ran their code in the background every
frame. That code is now removed, which makes the game's first download a little smaller (about 12 KB compressed) and
takes some work off every frame.

**Four old modes are gone.** Walk the arena, Gun Run, Crown and Search belonged to the old game and never appeared in
SpeedKills. They and their code are removed. The arena modes left are team deathmatch, free-for-all and Control.

**Resurgence is gone.** The old game's alternative battle royale rules, where the dead dropped back in from the sky,
are removed. SpeedKills never offered them; its own way back is a squad mate restoring your ghost at your echo.
