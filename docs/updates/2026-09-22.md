# 22 September 2026

## The clothes were not covering the body

The owner said it plainly and they were right. Two separate faults, and neither of them was visible from
reading the code.

A garment file holds more than one skinned mesh. The Ranger body file is a coat and two belts. We took the
first skinned mesh we found and dropped the rest, which is why a figure could turn up wearing a belt on a
bare chest. Every mesh in the file is worn now.

The second is worse and cannot be fully fixed with code. The garments reference `T_Regular_Male_*`: they
were modelled on the pack's Regular proportions, and the free tier of Universal Base Characters ships only
Superhero, which is 424 mm across the shoulders against the grey mannequin's 384. A coat cut for the
slimmer build does not close on the bulkier one. So the torso is narrowed towards the shoulders before the
cloth goes on, and the cloth sits on a 22 mm shell above the skin so the two surfaces stop fighting over
the same pixels. The narrowing had to be kept off the arms: in the bind pose the arms lie out at shoulder
height, so narrowing everything at that height pulled the hands in off their own wrist bones and the
skinning stretched them into fans.

Screenshots found all of this, and screenshots are easier to take now: the gun in your own hands can be put
away for a shot, which used to cover the half of a figure nearest the camera.

## Nobody is bald any more

Four hairstyles and a beard came down with the bodies weeks ago and have been sitting in
`public/models/body/hair` worn by nobody, which is why every figure in every screenshot had a shaved head.
They are rigged to the same 65 joints as everything else, so they are fetched and worn by the same path a
garment is. The pack ships them as greyscale masks - mean 143 of 255, measured off the webp - because it
expects the engine to colour them, so worn as they came every figure had white hair. Each outfit names a
colour now, and hair is kept out of the outfit's tint so a man in olive does not have olive hair.

## Twenty outfits, twenty outlines

The wardrobe was twelve shapes wearing twenty names, and shape is what a player reads first at the range
this game is played at. Forty part files allow sixty-four combinations per body, so every outfit takes its
own. The sets cut for the lighter body now wear the male garments: the pack's female tops are corsets with
bare shoulders and a low neckline, which is the asset rather than a fault, and a figure was turning up bare
from the collarbone down.

Mixing the two packs broke the recoloured atlases, which were one per outfit. The packs lay their UVs out
differently, so a peasant shirt reading a ranger atlas is a garment in somebody else's seams - which is
what the legs of half the wardrobe were. Twenty-two atlases now, one per outfit and pack.

## The free asset pool, searched to the bottom

The owner called free assets a gold mine next to modelling our own, and they are. Here is the whole mine.

Both Quaternius free tiers are entirely taken: two bodies and five hairstyles from Universal Base
Characters, Ranger and Peasant from Modular Character Outfits. The rest of both packs is $40, and that $40
buys the Regular body the garments are actually cut for, which would end the fit problem at its root rather
than working around it.

Sketchfab's CC0 downloadable rigged humans are museum scans - searched for soldier, swat, military uniform,
tactical vest, hoodie and camouflage, and the pool is empty. itch's CC0 character packs are fantasy or
blocky and each on its own rig, a step down from what we have. Poly Pizza mirrors Quaternius and Kenney.
ambientCG and Poly Haven have CC0 fabric and leather but no garments.

What is left is Mixamo, and it is the biggest of the lot: 108 rigged characters, free for unlimited
commercial use with no royalty and no attribution, including Swat, Swat Guy, Gas Mask, Vanguard, the
prisoner in coveralls and about sixty men and women in t-shirts, jackets and jeans. `npm run mixamo` lists
them and writes the catalogue; the list endpoint answers without a login. The export endpoint wants one
Adobe bearer token, and that is the only thing standing between this game and a modern wardrobe.
