# 29 September 2026

**Quieter spraying.** Your own tracers are now about a quarter as bright, so spraying the USSO no longer throws a sheet
of light over what you are shooting at, and your muzzle flash is drawn at half strength on every gun. Other players'
tracers are as bright as before, so you can still see where fire is coming from. None of the bought packs came with a
muzzle flash of its own, so the flash is still ours, just softer.

**The guns at rest, as they were.** Standing still, the USSO and BOOG now sit where they did before the new arms: the
same size in the corner of the screen and pointing level at the crosshair. The new arms had been drawing the gun with a
narrower view of their own, which made it bigger and lower and angled it up at the crosshair, and they held it 2 degrees
up on top of that.

**The new city has started: Neon City, laid out and playable.** The next SpeedKills city is being built from scratch
out of the Neon City bundle the owner bought, with nothing drawn by hand. Its centre is laid out: a 3 by 3 grid of
blocks with wide streets, a 151 m tower in the middle, High City's 26 m towers with big walkable roofs on the four
sides, and lower buildings of 3 to 6 storeys in the corners. The eight districts around it are plain pavement and roads
for now. It is not the default map yet: add `?map=neon` to the address to play it.

**Jump pads that look like they belong.** The new pads are the pack's own: a red metal plate in the street with a
blue searchlight beam rising from it, visible across the city. Eight of them throw you onto High City's roofs, and the
bots use them too.

**The pack's materials drawn the way Unity draws them.** The roads were coming out white and mirror-shiny: the pack
keeps a third of its surfaces in a second texture layer, and a roughness setting was being applied twice. Both are
fixed, so the asphalt, concrete and metal look like the pack's. The pack's light beams now glow instead of drawing as
solid white columns.

**No standing inside the walls.** High City's towers turned out to be empty shells, and you could slip in through the
gaps at their base and stand inside one. Now they are solid all the way up.

**Loot up high.** Each High City roof is a named place on the big map (North, South, West and East Deck) with loot of
its own, so landing on a roof is worth it.

**No walking on air over the tower's pit.** The new map's tallest tower has a basement 7 m below the street, and it
was standing in an open hole where you could see the sky through the ground and walk across on nothing. It now stands
in a sunken court: paved floor, concrete walls, and you can drop in, fight among its basement and stairs, and climb out
over the walls.

**Neon City is now SpeedKills' map.** No special address needed any more: SpeedKills opens on it. The old city is still
there at `?map=city` for comparison.

**Underground halls.** Four metro corridors now lead off the tower's sunken court under the plaza, lit, with posters and
shop fronts, somewhere to fight out of sight. The court's walls, two of which you could see straight through from inside,
are solid to look at now.

**Metro entrances.** Two metro kiosks, glass with the "U" sign, now stand on the plaza north and south of the tower.
Walk in, ride down the two escalators, and you come out in the underground corridor that leads into the tower's sunken
court; and back up the same way.

**Neon City at night looks like night.** The streets are dark and wet now, reflecting the city's lights instead of a
pale sky, and the map has its own violet haze on the horizon instead of the old city's coloured fogs. At golden hour the
roads reflect the golden city.

**Streets with life in them.** The new map's streets have the pack's street lamps now, parked cars along the kerbs to
crouch behind, and flying cars over the streets.

**Neon signs.** The buildings along the centre's streets now carry the pack's glowing signs, "holocall", "COOL5", the
tall Korean ones and more.

**Still to come for this map:** the signs, holograms and street props the store pictures are full of, the haze and
neon lighting, the metro under the middle, the tower's rooms to fight in, and bridges between the roofs.

**Swapping by throwing.** Switching between the USSO and BOOG, your hands now lift the gun and toss it up and away.
It turns over in the air and burns out, and the next gun burns in out there and flies back into your hands, which come
up to catch it. It takes the same time a swap always has. The old swap, where the gun swung down to your chest, is
still one setting away if you prefer it.

**The USSO's sight whole when aiming.** Aiming down the USSO, part of the sight looked see-through and flickered. The
back of the gun was coming right up against your eye, closer than the game draws, so its back end was being cut off.
The gun now sits a few centimetres further out when aimed (on any gun whose back would reach the eye), and the sight
stays whole while you fire.

**A better look at your hacks.** Inspecting your gun, your other hand now comes up from below, lower and closer, instead
of the whole arm sticking out from the side of the screen. The hack cards over your palm are bigger and a little further
right, glow softly, and show each hack's level as the same coloured pips as the boxes at the bottom of the screen. The
inspect lasts two seconds longer, holding a moment after each turn of the gun, and near the end you toss the cards up
and they burn out in the air the way a gun does when you swap.

**Everyone starts with the USSO and BOOG.** The first loadout is now the USSO and BOOG, the two guns finished all the
way, in your hands and on your soldier. New players start with them, and anyone who played before is moved onto them
once. If they pick something else after that, it stays picked.

**Who played, and when.** Until now the server could not say whether a friend had been on: it kept no names, and the
boards only hear about wins and course times (USSO's last, as "Usso", was on the 21st). The game now leaves a short line
in a log on the server when someone opens the page, makes or joins a lobby, starts a match and closes the page. Each
line has the name they play under and an id their browser made for itself; their address is only kept scrambled.
`npm run fps seen` prints the last three days as visits, like "Mon 8:00 PM to 8:24 PM, Usso: opened an invite, joined
the lobby, played a 1v1, played a battle royale, closed it after 24 min". The deploy's own test browser is left out.

**Enemies' hands on their guns.** Up close, the soldier holding the USSO looked like it was gripping nothing: its right
hand crossed through the grip, and on BOOG it lay flat against the side of the gun with the fingers spread. Now each
hand wraps its gun the way a hand does: the right round the pistol grip with the fingers through the trigger guard and
the thumb round the far side, the index finger straight along the frame; the left under the front with its fingers
together up the side. The soldier's gloved hands are big, and the USSO's trigger guard was too small for four of those
fingers, so the USSO an enemy carries is drawn a tenth bigger (yours is unchanged). BOOG is long enough that a hand
cannot reach its front, so the soldier now stands more side-on with it, as a marksman does, and holds it by its
magazine. Standing, aiming and crouching with the USSO now look right from every side; the rest (the reload, the swap,
running, BOOG's forearm against its stock) is next.

**The USSO's reload, hands on.** Racking the USSO, your left hand now closes around the charging handle, fingers curled
over the top of the gun, instead of resting flat against its side. And while you point at the magazine, your finger now
moves a moment before it: the finger flicks down and the magazine drops out after it, then the finger lifts and the new
one rises in behind it, so it looks like you are steering it.

**Invite once, then take everyone with you.** Making a lobby now leaves you in the range while you wait, not the small
1v1 map, with the code on your screen. When your friend opens the link, the match starts as it always has. From then on
you are a group. In a match or out of one, pick any mode and the button reads "Start for everyone (2)": everyone goes
straight into it, mid-1v1 or mid-battle royale included, with no new code. Your friend's button just says the host
picks.

**The range, together.** The firing range is now a mode you can play with friends: you all stand in it as you would
alone, see each other, hear each other's shots and see each other's hacks and emotes, and nobody can hurt anyone. It
is where a group lands after a battle royale ends, so the host can pick the next match from there. A friend who opens
your old invite link while you are in the range walks in beside you.

**Deploys can no longer call a broken release live.** The check the deploy runs at the end, a real 1v1 on the live
site, reported success even when that 1v1 failed part way, because of a mistake in how it finished. It now fails the
deploy. The one failure it has shown twice, a friend's first Join right after a deploy being told there is no such
match, is tried once more and reported, so it cannot hide a real problem either.
