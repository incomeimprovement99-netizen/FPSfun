# 27 September 2026

A15 and A16 went live at 7ccc614, on the game server (its live check passed) and on Pages. Then the full release
e2e ran on the live build: the mixed batch passed, and the other two failed six checks between them, all in the
legacy game: the course's clock, a door's second kick, and four checks on the vault. Every one passed when its
section ran alone. The machine was at 95% while they ran (the e2e's own browser on about eleven cores, and the
footage measuring for A17 on one more), and a starved page's game time falls behind the wall's, since each frame's
step is capped at 0.1 s: the door's second kick came inside the first's cooldown, and the vault's prompt was read
before the frame that moved you there had run. The e2e now has `gameSleep`, which waits on the game's clock, and
those checks use it.

A17 started with the part that does not wait on footage: a check that the city's roofs join up
(`tools/checks/sk-roofrun.ts`). It finds the city's 186 roofs and, from each toward every roof it faces across a
gap, runs the real movement controller over the real city: sprint, jump at the edge, double jump at the top of the
arc. At today's movement 147 of the 958 facing gaps are crossed, the widest 36 m (a drop from a high roof carries
further), and the best roof reaches 9 others without touching the street. With the first pass's sprint put back
it is 99 crossings and 7 roofs, so A15 opened about half again as many jumps across the city. The check holds
today's numbers a tenth under, so a change that breaks the flow fails, and A17's footage numbers will move them
with their reason.

While A17 waits on the footage, A18's instrument went in. The game loop now marks its phases (`?perf`), and a
frame over 50 ms keeps which phase it was in, so a hitch names its cause, or is shown to be outside the loop. The
bench repeats every preset in turn and reports the median and the spread, since one run is not a measurement on
a machine doing other things, and it has a new spot, a sprint down a street toward the Spire in a full match. A
first run says render is most of a frame (5.5 ms of 7.8 at Competitive) and found no hitch, but it ran beside the
footage work, so the real numbers come once the machine is quiet.

The first thing A18 turned up was a fault A15 made. The host checks every hit a friend claims, and it refused a
swing when it saw the two more than 5 m apart. But the swinger hit the other where they were drawn, up to 0.3 s
behind plus a network trip, and at the new top speed a target covers 7 m in that time: a real swing could be 9.4 m
apart at the host, and it was thrown away. SpeedKills' limit is now worked out from the swing's reach, the top
speed and the buffer (Milestone 236), and the check that holds it fails with the old 5 m put back.

The same working-out found the bots' side of it. A bot's aim follows you late by its tier's lag, so it trails a
runner by the runner's speed times the lag; doubling your speed doubled that for every tier, and a Skilled bot
that aimed 1.2 m behind a legacy sprint was 2.6 m behind a SpeedKills one. The bots now aim with half the lag in
SpeedKills, which puts each tier back where it was tuned (Milestone 237); it is one number in speedkills.json if
easier bots are wanted.

And one thing tried and taken back out. A friend's figure at the new speed moves as evenly as before for a typical
frame, but its worst frames are rougher in the test. The obvious suspect was the figure carrying on past its newest
state and snapping back when the next one lands, so a glide back was built and tested against it, four runs each
way, interleaved: it made no difference, so it is not the cause, and it is not shipped. The numbers are in the plan,
to be taken again on a quiet machine, and the real judge is a match with friends.
