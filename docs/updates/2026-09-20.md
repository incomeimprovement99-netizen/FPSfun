# 2026-09-20

A long day: two performance and correctness milestones, the bots' last bit of
traversal, and then the title card, twice.

## Shipped

**Milestone 125, the bots ride the balloons.** The last of the map's ways
across it that the bots did not use. A bot standing at a jump tower with more
than 110 m still to go rides the balloon and glides for its goal, the way you
do; under that it walks, because the ride costs it the climb and the fall. Not
while it has someone to fight, and a follower only takes a tower its lead is
already past, so a squad is not scattered by one.

**Milestone 126, the world drawn as far as the preset says.** The camera's far
plane was a flat 400 m while the open map's fog ran to 680 and its corners are
622 m apart, so a ridge in clear air was cut off at a seam that travelled with
you. Each preset now has its own draw distance (Competitive 460 m, Balanced
620, High 760), the fog ends exactly there, and the camera reaches 60 m past
the fog. Seeing further had to mean drawing more, or the setting would only
have moved the fog: the distances the field's rock, scrub and cliff faces are
drawn to now follow the preset, which takes Competitive from 598k triangles to
391k at the map's corner.

Two things were found while measuring it:

- **The benchmark had been measuring the battle royale map under the firing
  range's short fog.** Nothing on its map spots starts a match, and the fog
  follows the region you are playing in, so every number ever taken on that map
  was taken with a 290 m fog over it. The spots say which region they are in
  now, and there is a `brcorner` spot for the longest sightline in the game.
- **The battle royale had no sky.** A new picture of the map's corner came back
  black above the horizon. The dome is built with the firing range, and the
  region split of Milestone 100 hid it the moment you were on the map; the fog
  still took its colour, so the ground faded into haze and nothing read as
  broken from inside a match. Nineteen milestones of battle royale were played
  under a black sky. It is over the whole world now, with a check so it cannot
  go again.

**Milestone 127, the title card.** B00G's FPS: FULL POWER SURGE. Green rain
falling at its own speed in every column, the name smashed in over it from two
and a half times its size with a green and a cyan copy a few pixels either
side, then a shot through the middle of the screen: a flash, a kick, a bullet
hole, thirteen cracks out to the edges with rings of glass between them, and
the pane falling away in shards into the game behind it.

**Milestone 128, the owner's notes on it.** The loading screen is gone from
under the card: it starts on the first drawn frame, the bar and the tip step
off the page, and how much of the world is in is the line under the name. The
card holds at the moment of the shot until the world is in, so the animation
covers loading instead of following it. Three seconds longer, and after the
first round a beat of quiet and then the rest of the magazine: eleven rounds,
each its own hole with its own short cracks and its own kick, before the pane
lets go.

## Later the same day

**Milestone 129, the crosshair over the shoulder and a red dot on every
loadout.** Aiming in third person took the crosshair away and left nothing to
aim with, because the rule that hides it (the gun's sights replace it) is a
first-person rule. And every loadout's guns now come fitted with the 1x holo,
or whatever 1x that gun can take: nobody builds a class and leaves it on irons.
A gun with a scope of its own keeps it, and a battle royale's floor loot is
untouched.

**Milestone 130, PAINT.** Two bombs in the grenade slots that splash a patch
over whatever they hit. Orange: a third more top speed and half again the
acceleration while you are on it. Blue: a jump off it goes 1.6 times as high.
The carry is the mechanic: the speed boost decays over 1.2 s after you leave
the patch, so a run over the orange into a slide into a jump is one movement
instead of four. A sprint on the paint measures 1.33x a sprint, and a
slide-jump off the end of a patch leaves at 337 hu/s against a 260 hu/s sprint.
With no paint down, every Apex number is exactly what it was.

Also written: the ranked list against AAA shooters (twenty items, with the
three things that still separate this from a shipped game named plainly), the
plan the paint came out of, and this diary.

**Milestone 131, the camera that moves with the body.** The movement numbers
were already Apex's rule by rule; what was missing was everything between the
inputs. A slide leans the view into the way it is carrying you, a running
landing rolls it, a lurch kicks it round a touch and settles, and a paint boost
pulls it forward with the view a little wider. All small, all gone inside a
third of a second, and none of it moves the aim: the e2e drives a real diagonal
slide in a real page and checks that the camera leans to 98% of its lean, comes
back to nothing, and that the angles the shot uses do not move at all. Sprint
view shake is the switch for the lot.

**Milestone 133, the owner's notes on the card again.** The rain opened by
drawing the word "undefined" (each column's glyphs were only filled on the
first swap, and until then the canvas printed the missing character as a word).
The eleven rounds after the first shot read as a strobe, so they are one
shotgun blast now: fourteen pellets through the pane in the same instant, one
flash, one kick, each with its own small break, with the first rifle round
still the break that carries the glass. And the card is 1.8 s shorter for it,
3.9 s from 5.7.
