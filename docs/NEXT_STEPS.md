# Next steps: the gap analysis, and the list that comes out of it

**Written 2026-09-16.** The owner asked for a thorough gap analysis against the big shooters and at least 40 next steps. This is that list: **70 items**, ranked, each with an effort in hours or days for one developer.

It came from twenty agents run in parallel: four read this repository (the battle royale map, the view model, the whole feature set, every asset and its licence) and fourteen researched Apex Legends, Warzone, Fortnite, PUBG and Tarkov, The Finals and Titanfall 2, Valorant, CS2 and Siege, gunplay feel, HUD and UX, audio, progression, bots and AI, netcode, and free CC0 asset sources. A synthesis pass merged about 150 raw findings into the ranked list; a completeness critic then asked what was still missing and added eighteen more.

Older analyses, still true where they are not superseded: `docs/GAP_ANALYSIS.md` (Apex Season 30 and Hyper Scape, per area) and `docs/HYPER_SCAPE.md` (what we took from Hyper Scape and what we forgot).

## What the analysis concluded

- The map is a diorama, not a level. Outskirts is 193,600 m2 of flat plane with five identical box yards on a perfect wheel: ~95% empty, one piece of cover per 3,000 m2, nothing above 13.5 m, no terrain, no landmarks, no mid-band. Every other BR problem — dead rotations, open-sand end games, bots that drift, loot that means nothing — is downstream of the layout, so the map items (interiors, connective tissue, verticality, silhouettes, terrain) are the backbone of the next phase.
- The expensive work is already built and sitting unused. Walk-in interiors with stairs and balconies that hold zero loot (floorAt rejects them outright); building() returning { roof, floors } 'so a caller can put loot on them' with no caller; loadouts.ts ignored by BR; buildLadder never called on Outskirts; staticmerge.ts not applied to the map; the Control capture code, the killcam camera rig and the care-package fall all reusable. A whole phase of high-value items is five to eight hours each because the system underneath is finished.
- Bugs and dead systems cluster where nothing tests them. The viewmodel arms, the two nav-graph traps, the out-of-bounds ring and the empty interiors all shipped silently because no tool looks at arm placement, node validity, ring bounds or loot reachability. Two cheap tooling items (a map self-check in verify.ts, a viewmodel screen-coverage guard in snap/e2e) convert this from a recurring class into a solved one.
- Fix causes, not symptoms. The forearm-in-the-face is elbow anchors stored in gun-local space riding a pose that pivots about the eye — already patched once locally for ADS, already worse on the flourish, and it will recur on every future pose until the anchors move to camera space. The same shape appears in the map: the ridge is tuned to within centimetres of movement constants, and the ring picks circles with no knowledge of the map at all.
- The bots are furniture-blind. They cannot jump, climb or slide, cannot loot, cannot open anything, cannot use a zipline, balloon, pad or beacon, see only 60 m on a map made of 165 m sightlines, and navigate 19 outdoor nodes at ground level. Every building and traversal prop added by the map work is unopposed until the AI can enter and ride it — so the nav-graph and bot-behaviour items must ship alongside the map items, not after them.
- Free CC0 assets are the cheapest fidelity available, and the pipeline is already proven. ambientCG textures, Poly Haven props and HDRIs, Quaternius animations and nature, Poly Pizza models and Kenney sounds all ship today with fetch scripts, a trim tool, a compress step and ATTRIBUTION files. Per-POI materials, real rocks, extra skies, door and bin SFX, and a proper HUD type and icon set are each half a day and each visible in every screenshot.
- Eight friends on a ten-minute BR need a shorter loop and a way back in. A bad landing currently costs the whole match with nothing to watch afterwards; Resurgence rules, spectating, self-revive, replicators and (eventually) a gulag all attack the same thing. Pair that with per-match variety — a hot zone, a dropship line, seeded chests, a different sky, a dust storm — so repeat matches on one map stop feeling identical.

## Free assets worth taking

Every one is checked for licence. The repo already fetches ambientCG, Poly Haven, Quaternius and Kenney with scripts, a trim tool, a compress step and ATTRIBUTION files, so each of these is half a day of plumbing rather than a new pipeline.

- ambientCG (CC0 1.0, public domain, no attribution required) — PBR material sets for per-POI palettes (rusted metal, corrugated sheet, poured concrete, road paint, sandstone, gravel, plaster, roof tile) and two ground blends for the 440 m plane. Already the source of public/tex; fetched by tools/fetch-assets.ts.
- Poly Haven (CC0 1.0) — rock and cliff scans to replace the 35 box rocks, more industrial props for POI dressing, and three or four extra HDRIs (clear noon, hazy afternoon, dusk, overcast) for a seeded per-match sky. Already the source of public/models (Barrel_01, steel_frame_shelves_01, portable_generator, concrete_road_barrier, modular_chainlink_fence) and public/tex/sky.hdr.
- Quaternius (CC0 1.0) — Ultimate Nature Pack for scrub, dead trees and boulders, and further clips from Universal Animation Library 1 and 2 for door interacts, vaulting and crawling. Already the source of the mannequin and its 25 clips (public/models/mannequin, 4.3 MB after tools/trim-glb.ts).
- Kenney (CC0 1.0) — audio packs (Impact Sounds, Interface Sounds, Sci-Fi Sounds) for door swings, latches, kicks, splinters, bin lids, console beeps and the supply-drop horn; plus the City and Industrial model kits for interior dressing (shelving, pallets, lockers, pipes, railings, signage). Already the source of public/audio/kenney via npm run sounds.
- Freesound, filtered to CC0 1.0 only (the site mixes CC0, CC-BY and CC-BY-NC — take CC0 and record the sound ids) — the few takes Kenney does not cover: a real door slam, a distant aircraft horn, a falling-canopy whistle, a gas-mask breath loop.
- Poly Pizza, filtered to CC0 (it also hosts CC-BY models; the repo already credits CreativeTrio's katana and a Quaternius dagger from here) — small one-off props and any further heirloom or melee models.
- KayKit on itch.io (CC0 1.0) — modular building, dungeon and prop kits; the cleanest single source for consistent interior furniture at a low poly budget that matches the existing look.
- game-icons.net (CC BY 3.0 — needs one attribution line in public ATTRIBUTION.md and the README) — a consistent HUD icon set for ammo types, heals, throwables, helmets, hop-ups, doors, bins, contracts and the map legend.
- Google Fonts, self-hosted (SIL OFL 1.1) — a condensed display face plus a clean UI face for the HUD (Barlow Condensed / Teko / Rajdhani are the natural pairings for this style); self-host into public/fonts so the game makes no third-party request and works offline behind Caddy.
- Lucide (ISC licence, permissive, attribution-free) — plain UI glyphs for the menus, settings tabs and the Friends lobby, where game-icons.net's illustrative style would be too heavy.
- Avoid: Sketchfab's CC-BY knife and weapon models (account required and attribution obligations the repo already declined), CS:GO/Apex replica models of any kind (they carry Valve's and Respawn's designs regardless of the uploader's licence tag), and anything labelled CC0 by an AI-generation site with unclear provenance — the repo already made and recorded these calls in docs/RESULTS_1V1_BETA.md.
